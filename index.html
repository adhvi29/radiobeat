Why it matters

The most dangerous heartbeat
is the one nobody feels.

Atrial fibrillation is an irregular rhythm that often comes and goes in silence, frequently at night. It can sharply raise the risk of stroke, yet many people never notice a single episode.

Being straight about it

What it can do,
and what it can't.

RadioBeat senses movement, not electricity. Knowing exactly where that line sits matters more than pretending it isn't there.

What it can do

  • Notice when beat-to-beat timing turns irregular, which is one of the main signs of AFib.
  • Track breathing rate through the night without anything worn.
  • Run in the dark, all night, with nothing to charge or remember.
  • Tell someone to go and get properly checked.

What it can't do

  • Replace an ECG. An ECG reads the heart's electrical signal through electrodes. This reads chest movement, so there is no P wave to record.
  • Diagnose anything. That takes a doctor and a proper test. This only raises a flag.
  • Cope with a busy room. Someone walking past shifts the signal far more than a heartbeat does.
  • Follow two people at once, at least not in this version.

It is a prompt to go get checked. Not a verdict.
